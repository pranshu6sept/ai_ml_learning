# Payments FAQ starter set

This file is intentionally a small starter set of questions written from public payment and banking
knowledge. It will later be expanded into more structured corpus entries.

## FAQ 1: What is the difference between authorization and settlement?

Authorization is the approval step that checks whether a payment instrument can be used and whether
the account has sufficient funds or approval for the transaction. Settlement is the later process where
funds move between parties after the transaction is accepted and processed.

## FAQ 2: What is ISO 20022?

ISO 20022 is a global messaging standard for payment and financial data. It standardizes the format
of messages so institutions can exchange structured payment information more consistently.

## FAQ 3: Why is strong customer authentication important?

Strong customer authentication reduces fraud by adding a second layer of verification before a
payment or sensitive operation is approved. It helps ensure the person performing the transaction is
actually the legitimate account holder.

## FAQ 4: What is PCI DSS?

PCI DSS is a security standard for protecting payment card data. It provides requirements for storing,
transmitting, and processing cardholder data securely.

## FAQ 5: What is a dispute in card payments?

A dispute happens when a cardholder challenges a transaction and asks for investigation, reversal, or
customer protection due to issues such as fraud, non-receipt, or billing problems.

## FAQ 6: Why do payment systems need good chunking and retrieval?

Payment documentation is dense, structured, and often spread across standards, rules, and guidance.
Good retrieval depends on preserving section boundaries and technical context so the system can answer
questions grounded in the correct policy or rule.
