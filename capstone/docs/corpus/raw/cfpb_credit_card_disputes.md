# CFPB guidance: disputing a credit card charge (United States)

## Public summary

The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains how a cardholder can dispute a charge on a credit card
bill. This is a consumer-law process between the cardholder and the card company. It is separate from the
card-scheme chargeback rules between issuing and acquiring banks, which this guidance does not cover. The
related rule is Regulation Z, section 1026.13, billing error resolution.

## How to dispute

Call the card company about the problem right away. To protect your rights, you must also send a written billing
error notice to the card company within 60 calendar days after the charge appeared on your statement. The
notice should give your name, address and account number and clearly explain what you think is wrong and why.
Keep copies of all correspondence and a record of the dates of follow-up calls. You can dispute a charge even if
you have already paid it, although reimbursement may take longer.

## What the card company must do

After receiving the notice, the company has 30 days to send a letter confirming that it received the dispute,
unless it has already finished the resolution process within that time. If you are correct, the charge must be
removed from your bill. If the company says the bill is correct, it must explain why in writing and state the
amount owed and the payment due date.

## During the investigation

While a dispute is open, the company may not report the payment as late to a credit reporting company if you
paid the undisputed amount on time, charge interest on the disputed amount, or make you pay the disputed charge.

## Other cases

A different process may apply if you are disputing charges for defective goods or services.
