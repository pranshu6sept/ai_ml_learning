# RBI card-not-present transactions and additional authentication (2016, repealed)

> **Status: historical.** The RBI repealed this circular through its Authentication mechanisms for digital payment
> transactions Directions, 2025 (Annexure 2). Do not treat the figures below as current rules.

## Public summary

This is a Reserve Bank of India circular dated 6 December 2016 (reference RBI/2016-17/172) on card-not-present
(CNP) transactions. A card-not-present transaction is an online payment where the physical card is not
presented. Normally such a transaction needs an additional factor of authentication (AFA) beyond the card
details. The circular relaxes that requirement for small payments under conditions.

## The relaxation

The AFA requirement is relaxed for online card-not-present transactions up to ₹2,000, when the payment is made
through a payment authentication solution provided by an authorised card network together with participating
issuing and acquiring banks. The cardholder registers once, giving the card details and completing AFA with
the issuing bank. Afterwards the registered card details act as the first factor and the login credentials of
the card network act as the second factor. Above ₹2,000, AFA remains mandatory.

## Conditions and responsibilities

- The cardholder's consent is required, and the solution is optional for card-not-present transactions up to ₹2,000.
- Banks should educate customers about how to use the solution, its risks and how to raise grievances, and
  should disclose the maximum customer liability at enrolment.
- Velocity checks, meaning limits on how often transactions can happen, are recommended.
- Banks and networks are listed as bearing full liability for any security breach or compromise.
- The existing chargeback process does not change.

## Authority, scope and date

The circular was issued under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 (sections 10(2) and 18). It is a 2016
document and it has since been repealed: the Reserve Bank of India Directions of 25 September 2025 list it in
Annexure 2 among the circulars they repeal, so the rules and limits described here no longer apply.
